# Minimal Orbital Thrustcraft

*A symmetric 5-block Autopilot design retained as a distinct performance–cost point rather than discarded after the later 3-block minimization.*

## Track

## Run Summary

`Minimal Orbital Thrustcraft` is a 5-block machine consisting of one Starting Block and four Steering Thrusters arranged symmetrically around the core. It was generated through the BuildArena 2.0 MCP by Codex Desktop using GPT-5.6 Luna in Autopilot mode, then legally tuned and manually piloted for the submitted run.

The 5-block run is **Part II** of the linked video. Independent analysis of the submitted trajectory gives approximately:

- three orbital cycles in **73.31 s**;
- five cycles in **131.13 s**;
- about **5.05 cumulative cycles** over the full recording;
- full-run effective angular rate of about **0.2398 rad/s**;
- **100% structural integrity** throughout the recorded run.

These values are diagnostic measurements from the submitted CSV, not an official BuildArena score.

## Why This Entry Was Retained

A later GPT-5.6 Luna Autopilot build produced the separate 3-block `MinimumCostOrbitalOpposedPair`. It would have been easy to treat that later machine as a simple replacement for this one, but the two machines optimize different sides of the current scoring problem.

The S01 performance metric rewards:

```text
70% Orbit Progress
20% Speed Score
10% Structure Integrity
```

and the final score also incorporates machine/token cost and the build-mode coefficient.

The 5-block machine uses two more Steering Thrusters than the 3-block machine, but the recorded run is also faster. The 3-block machine is mechanically more minimal, while the 5-block machine provides more actuation/control margin. With the exact unified scoring implementation resolved only during judging, both designs are meaningful candidates rather than duplicates.

For that reason, we retained both as separate submissions: they form two distinct points in a performance–cost trade-off produced under the same model and agent environment.

## Scoring Interpretation and Flight Strategy

The design process was guided by a direct reading of the published scoring objective: orbital progress alone is not the full performance target. Speed is explicitly rewarded.

We therefore treated angular rate as an engineering variable rather than considering orbit insertion to be the endpoint of the run. The objective was not simply to establish a stable orbit, but to continue generating valid angular progress as efficiently as possible while preserving structural integrity.

This led to an actively piloted, thrust-supported trajectory rather than a purely passive coast. Stability remained important because the machine still had to maintain valid progress and stay intact, but it was treated as a means of sustaining performance rather than as the terminal objective itself.

The 5-block configuration represents the more conservative end of this strategy. Its four symmetric actuators provide greater control authority and correction margin while still avoiding fuel tanks, conventional rocket engines, dedicated stabilization blocks, and additional structural framing.

## Architecture

The raw machine contains:

- 1 Starting Block;
- 4 Steering Thrusters in two mirrored opposed pairs.

The Starting Block is the complete structural core. The four Steering Thrusters provide both propulsion and differential control without a fuel system. The symmetry gives the pilot more independent corrective authority than the later two-thruster design.

After the raw save, only vanilla-legal non-geometric parameters and control bindings were tuned. The submitted machine_tuned.bsg reconstructs the tuned configuration used for the recorded run. No structural geometry was manually changed.

## A Same-Model Comparison: Why GPT-5.6 Luna Produced 5P First and 3P Later

Both submitted machines were generated by **GPT-5.6 Luna in Codex Desktop**, using the same isolated workspace and the same BuildArena MCP. This makes their architectural difference useful as a prompt-sensitivity observation.

The prompt for this 5-block build asked for the lowest-cost *practical* orbital machine, emphasized symmetry, and asked the model to retain enough controllability for reliable human flight. Under that broader specification, the model chose four identical Steering Thrusters around the Starting Block. This is a reasonable engineering prior: symmetric redundancy reduces control risk.

The later 3-block prompt changed the priors. It explicitly accepted a single principal differential steering axis, aggressively penalized redundant actuators, focused the search on opposed fuel-free thrusters, and reminded the model that legal post-build parameter tuning meant that weak default settings should not automatically disqualify a geometry.

The result was a reduction from four Steering Thrusters to two.

This comparison is important to our interpretation of agent engineering. The model did not simply converge to one immutable “best” machine. **The architecture depended on which physical and operational assumptions were made explicit in the prompt.** Broad requests for practicality encouraged redundancy; explicit permission to sacrifice control authority for the scored horizon encouraged minimalism.

The full prompts and BuildArena MCP interactions for this entry are preserved in `chat_transcript.md`.
